Fear to Folklore: Erosion's Impact on
Allegheny Plateau Folk Culture (2026)

Independent project led by Madeleine Wagshul. Mentored by John Frazier, M.A.


Supported, in part, by Humanities Edge, the Mellon Foundation, and Miami Dade College.


Presented at the 2026 Humanities Edge Interdisciplinary Symposium.

Fear to Folklore: Erosion's Impact on Allegheny Plateau Folk Culture is an interdisciplinary analysis of cultural trends within the heavily eroded Allegheny Plateau region of western Appalachia.


This project connects quantitative geographic and geological data with qualitative cultural data, ultimately arguing in favor of geography's connection to folklore and the importance of preserving cultural identity.

The Natural Way of Us
A Cross-Temporal Analysis of Human Complexities In Feminist Literature (2025)

Independent project led by Madeleine Wagshul. Mentored by John Frazier, M.A.


Supported, in part, by Humanities Edge, the Mellon Foundation, and Miami Dade College.


Presented at the 2025 Humanities Edge Interdisciplinary Symposium and 2026 Johns Hopkins Macksey Symposium. Accepted into the 2026 National Council Undergraduate Research (NCUR) Symposium.

The Natural Way of Us: A Cross-Temporal Analysis of Human Complexities In Feminist Literature is a comparative analysis between Lysistrata (411 B.C.E.) by Aristophanes and The Natural Way of Things (2015 C.E.) by Charlotte Wood. This project seeks to establish contrasts and similarities between feminist texts from vastly differing eras.

Although findings are interpreted through an anthropological and artistic lens, research and methodology incorporate additional disciplines–particularly geography, political science, history, philosophy, sociology, psychology, and human rights studies. Ultimately, this project seeks to expand upon themes of power dynamics and unification within feminist texts and apply them more broadly to the human experience through the use of visual language and interdisciplinary analysis.

Despite The Natural Way of Us including supplementary essays, its primary component is a series of artworks exploring subsequent findings. The conclusion of this project addresses individual and collective liberation, both through philosophical methodologies that encourage critical analysis within the audience, and a written segment of my personal self-reflection.

Janie Speaks (2025)

Collaborative project led by Dr. Sabrina Walters, PhD. Mentored by Dr. Sabrina Walters, PhD


Supported, in part, by Humanities Edge, the Mellon Foundation, and Miami Dade College.


Presented at independent Janie Speaks gallery.

Janie Speaks is a collaborative humanities research project led and mentored by Dr. Sabrina Walters, PhD, of Miami Dade College North Campus.

In this projects, students were encouraged to create artistic media based on a literary analysis of Their Eyes Were Watching God by Zora Neale Hurston. The project introduced students to a multidisciplinary lens to research and analysis skills.
